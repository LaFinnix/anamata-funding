# Anamata Records — Funding Application Style Guide

Synthesised from the 2026 Creative NZ Development Fund application
(successful, $10,000) and the People & Organisations supporting doc.
Update this when more past applications are analysed.

## The voice

**Formal-policy-meets-kaupapa.** Not academic. Not casual. Long but
disciplined sentences. Reads like a serious funder submission.

**Avoid:** generic arts-grant boilerplate. Don't say "empowering
communities," "celebrating culture," "honouring tradition." Ngāika
didn't.

**Use:** Te Tiriti o Waitangi, te ao Māori, tikanga, rangatiratanga,
kaitiakitanga, tāngata whaikaha Māori, Māori futurism, trilingual,
universal design, vibrotactile, haptic-ready.

## The framing line (always)

> Anamata Records — driving the Māori futurism movement in Aotearoa's
> music industry.

Use this (or a close variant) in every bio, every application, every
intro paragraph. It positions Anamata in a specific lane that
funders recognise.

## The two-pillar pattern (winning structure)

Successful Creative NZ application was built on **two parallel
pillars**. This worked because:

- It's memorable (funder can summarise it in one sentence).
- It signals both cultural depth AND contemporary relevance.
- It survives scrutiny — both pillars had real deliverables.

When applying to anything, try to frame the work as **Pillar 1 +
Pillar 2** where each pillar has:
- A clear noun-phrase title (3 words max)
- A real deliverable
- A named partner organisation
- A budget line

## The three-phase delivery arc

**Phase I — Assessment (months 1–3):** gap analysis, baseline audit,
external consultants contracted.

**Phase II — Upskilling (months 4–9):** training delivery, technical
trials, prototype development.

**Phase III — Codification (months 10–12):** policies written,
advisory board stood up, public charter/plan published.

This arc works because it's **assess → train → embed**. Funders love
it because the deliverable in Phase III is permanent institutional
change, not just an event.

## The budget pattern

| Category | Typical % | What goes here |
|---|---|---|
| Professional Fees | ~40% | External consultants, contractors |
| Training and Education | ~18% | NZQA / formal credentials |
| Digital and Tech Development | ~22% | Audits, software, accessibility tools |
| Community Engagement | ~15% | Focus groups, koha, interpreters, catering |
| Materials and Resources | ~5% | Bilingual/trilingual documentation |

**Skews external.** Funders want to see money going to capability
(experts, training, tech) not salaries.

## The concrete-specificity rule

Every claim needs to be anchored to ONE of:
- A **named programme** (NZQA Level 4 Bicultural Competency,
  "Arts For All", Te Kaa, Te Tiriti)
- A **named organisation** (Arts Access Aotearoa, Maurea, Access
  Advisors, MMIC, SoundCheck Aotearoa, Otago Polytechnic)
- A **dollar amount** in the budget
- A **date or month range**
- A **statistic** (e.g. "25% of New Zealanders are Deaf or disabled")

If a paragraph has none of these, it's probably waffle. Rewrite.

## The People & Organisations pattern

When listing partners in an application:

- **One partner per section**, headed with name + organisation.
- **Role** (1 line)
- **Status** — only say "engaged / currently in contact" if true.
  Never list someone you haven't actually approached.
- **Description** — 2–3 sentences on what they bring, named
  programmes they run, why they're the right fit.

## The closing whakataukī

End applications with:

> "He aha te mea nui o te ao? He tāngata, he tāngata, he tāngata."
> What is the greatest thing in the world? It is people, it is
> people, it is people.

Translated. Always. Don't leave the whakataukī untranslated for a
non-Māori audience.

## Ngāika Smith's standing bio

> Ngāika Smith is a researcher and music producer with whakapapa to
> Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu, and Ngāti Awa, with a dedicated
> commitment to the Māori futurism movement in Aotearoa's music
> sector.

Re-use as-is, or adapt only the role (Project Manager / Lead
Researcher / Founder / Director).

## Don't lose these unique angles

When drafting future applications, keep at least ONE of these
differentiators on the page:

1. **Trilingual production** (English / te reo Māori / NZSL) — rare
   in NZ music; funders notice.
2. **Haptic-ready / vibrotactile music** — genuinely novel.
3. **Māori futurism as a movement** — positions Anamata as a leader,
   not just a participant.
4. **Tāngata whaikaha Māori as a specific audience** — not just
   "Deaf/disabled" but the culturally specific framing.
5. **Māori Music Industry Collective / SoundCheck Aotearoa
   integration** — shows sector connectedness.

## Common mistakes to avoid (based on this one application)

- **No evaluation metrics.** Future applications should specify how
  success will be measured (e.g. "X staff certified by month 9",
  "Y accessibility improvements shipped").
- **No risk section.** Brief risk register ("if consultant X pulls
  out, we have Y as backup") reads as mature.
- **Limited music-specific framing.** This was a capability grant so
  music was implicit. For production grants, foreground the actual
  waiata and artists.
