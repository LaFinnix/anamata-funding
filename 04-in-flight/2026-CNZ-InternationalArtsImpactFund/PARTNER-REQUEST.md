# What we need from the Japan partner
## Creative New Zealand International Arts Impact Fund 2026, Stage Two

**Deadline:** 1:00 PM NZ time, Thursday 1 October 2026. That is **9:00 AM the same day, Japan time**.
**Applicant:** Anamata Kāhui Limited, trading as Anamata Records (NZBN 9429052960734), Ngā toi Māori pool.
**Ask:** $75,000 per year for three years, fund period 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2029.
**Partner:** Ryo Suzuki, collaborating artist and partner to the organisation.

## What Creative New Zealand actually requires

The three support documents are mandatory, in the fund's own words:

1. Current International Strategy
2. Evidence of international demand, such as invitations
3. Brief artist biography or organisation profile that demonstrates previous international experience and capability

Everything below is ordered by what those three documents need, and by what makes each claim checkable rather than asserted.

## Tier 1. Needed this week, blocks the submission

### 1.1 A written invitation, on the partner's letterhead, signed and dated

This is the single strongest document in the whole application. It is the difference between "we have a Japanese partner" and "Japan has invited us".

What it must contain, or it will not count for an assessor:

- The entity or person's full legal name, address, email and phone. A letter on a company, studio or label letterhead beats a personal email signature.
- The relationship stated plainly: two bilingual releases already out, a third in production, and how long the collaboration has run.
- A specific commitment with dates, not a warm sentence. Examples that would carry weight: a presentation to Japanese industry in Tokyo in a named month of 2027; a co-writing residency; a named venue, festival or studio; a distribution or label commitment.
- What the partner brings, itemised: studio access, session players, marketing channels, local distribution, translation, industry introductions.
- What they understand the programme to be: the three year market programme, and the eight waiata where two are released and six are in development.
- A signature, the signer's name and role, and the date.
- **Japanese and English versions.** Japanese is the version that proves the commitment to a Japanese reader, English is the one we file. Say which is authoritative if they differ.
- For travel later: if we are to be in Japan, or the partner in Aotearoa, ask now for the visa-support documents the invitation implies (letter of reason, schedule of stay, invitee details). They are easier to obtain while the relationship is warm.

**Ask for this first, and ask for it today.** It is also the item a partner can produce in one hour once the text exists, which is why a draft is attached.

### 1.2 Partner information sheet, filled in

Facts we cannot invent and CNZ will expect to see in the profile and strategy:

- Full legal name of the entity, or the artist's full name as it appears on their ID.
- Japanese address, corporate number (法人番号) if there is one, and the representative's name and title.
- Website, social channels, and streaming profile links.
- Biography, roughly 200 words, and a shorter 50 word version for the profile document.
- Discography and any awards, with years.
- If the artist is signed to a label, publisher or management: who they are, and a one line confirmation that they are aware of this programme.
- Two or three referees or partners who can speak to their work, with contact details.
- High resolution portrait and any existing photography we may use, with permission in writing for use in the application.

### 1.3 A written statement on the current rights position of the released collaborations

The application states that masters, compositions and trade marks stay owned in Aotearoa New Zealand, and that the Japanese entity operates under an exclusive Japan territory licence. **For the two released collaborations, that claim has to be true in the way an assessor could check.**

So: one signed page, from the partner, stating for each of まだ終わっちゃいない and ただ声一つ:

- Who owns the master recording, and in what shares.
- Who owns the composition and lyrics, and in what shares.
- Who administers publishing in Japan, and with which society (JASRAC or NexTone, or none).
- Whether any third party (label, distributor, publisher) holds rights or an option over either recording.
- Whether the collaboration is exclusive or non exclusive for Japan.

If the existing splits are shared 50/50, say so. A shared past with a documented future structure is credible. A wrong claim discovered by an assessor is fatal.

## Tier 2. Needed within three days

### 2.1 A signed term sheet or letter of intent for the partnership going forward

Not a full contract, a signed page of principle. Terms to cover:

- Term, and the three year programme it applies to.
- The partner's role: creative role, and the named credits he expects.
- Revenue: the defined share of Japan territory revenue he receives, and whether he receives fees for services as well. The application says the partnership is contractual rather than equity, with no share of the masters, so this document is where that is either true or not.
- Territory and rights: Japan licence to the New Zealand vehicle, with the catalogue's ownership unchanged.
- Language and cultural protocol: how te reo Māori and Japanese sit in a work, who decides, and how the cultural review process on our side interacts with his.
- Approval rights on each side, in plain terms.
- What happens if either side stops, and how a work is retired or held.
- Governing law, and how disputes are handled.
- The structure he is contracting with: an acknowledgement that the counterparty will be the Japanese vehicle once formed, wholly owned by Anamata Kāhui Limited, with the terms transferring to it on formation, or a statement that he contracts with the New Zealand company instead.

### 2.2 Quotes in yen

The budget for three years cannot be built from guesses. Ask for quotes or written estimates for:

- Studio time, engineering, mixing and mastering in Japan, if any work is recorded there.
- Session players: taiko, shakuhachi, any others the programme names.
- Translation and localisation of lyrics, and interpreting for sessions.
- Marketing and PR in Japan, and any local distribution or aggregator fees.
- Registration and administration: JASRAC or NexTone, and any neighbouring rights body.
- Venue or presenter costs for the 2028 presentations.
- The partner's own fee or revenue share, stated separately from costs.
- Travel and accommodation for the 2028 Japan trip, and the inbound residency in Aotearoa.

Also ask for **the partner's own contribution**, cash or in kind, with a value. A partner who puts something in reads as a partner. It also strengthens the "international demand" claim more than any statement of enthusiasm.

### 2.3 Evidence of demand beyond the invitation

Anything already in the partner's hands:

- Streaming numbers by territory for the two released works, screenshots are fine.
- Japanese press, radio or playlist placements, with dates and links.
- Letters or emails from programmers, labels, publishers or promoters, even exploratory ones, dated.
- Audience or venue data he can share.
- Dates and names of any industry meetings already held with the label.

## Tier 3. Worth having before we file

- A short written statement from the partner on how he will hold the cultural side: te reo Māori and Japanese protocols in one room, and what he will not do. This is the cross Indigenous protocol the application promises to publish, so a paragraph from him now is the seed of it.
- Written confirmation on tax and payment mechanics: whether he or his entity will invoice, whether they are registered for Japanese consumption tax (the qualified invoice number, T plus thirteen digits), and what they need from us. We will also need a certificate of New Zealand residency from Inland Revenue for treaty relief on royalties, and the treaty rate for royalties should be confirmed with our accountant rather than assumed.
- Anything needed for travel: the invitation paperwork for visas in both directions, and proof of insurance arrangements.

## Tier 4. Ours to produce, not his. Named here so nothing is missed

- **The international strategy document does not exist yet.** The three year programme in the Stage One EOI is its substance. It needs to become a written strategy with market analysis, named partners and a budget. This is the largest single piece of work left and it is ours.
- **The organisation profile** can be assembled from the release record and the boilerplate.
- **The four application questions** have character limits: 5,000 for the first, 2,500 each for the other three.
- **The eligibility reply from Creative New Zealand** should be kept on file. The published exclusion catches artists and practitioners who have applied to the Creative Fellowship Fund for the same period; we applied to this fund as an organisation, which is a different applicant type for a different purpose. The published wording is defensible but worth having in writing.

## What makes this application hard to argue with

Not volume. Four things:

1. A Japanese partner who has put something in writing, on paper, with dates.
2. Rights stated accurately for the past, and structured deliberately for the future.
3. Numbers in yen that came from someone who will actually invoice.
4. A strategy that names the market, the partners and the sequence, rather than describing an intention.
