# Third-party support letters: templates

The partner does not need a registered studio for this to work, and it is often better that he does not rely
on one. What an assessor is looking for under "evidence of international demand, such as invitations" is
**independent parties** confirming that they will take part. A letter from a presenter or a venue is stronger
evidence than a letter from the collaborating artist, because it is a second party saying they will put
something in.

So the pack has two layers:

- **The partner letter** (already drafted): Ryo Suzuki confirming the creative partnership.
- **Role letters** below: from each organisation that will host, record, release or present the work. Studios
  do not have to be registered businesses to be booked, but if they want to appear as a partner they sign one
  of these, and a quote on their letterhead counts as evidence in its own right.

Priority order for weight with an assessor: **presenter or venue, festival or promoter, label or publisher,
studio, then any arts body or local authority.**

---

## Template A: presenter, venue, festival or promoter

> [Letterhead: organisation name, address, phone, email, website]
>
> [Date]
>
> Creative New Zealand
> International Arts Impact Fund assessment panel
> [by online submission]
>
> **Letter of support: Anamata Records Japan programme 2027 to 2029**
>
> To whom it may concern,
>
> I am [name], [role] at [organisation]. We [what the organisation does: present music, run a venue, programme
> a festival].
>
> I confirm that we are in discussion with Anamata Records, a trading name of Anamata Kāhui Limited in
> Aotearoa New Zealand, about presenting work from their Japan programme. [If a date is held or agreed, state
> it: "We have held [date or month] in our [year] programme for a performance by Ngaika Smith and his
> collaborating artists." If nothing is fixed, say what is true: "We have invited them to present in our
> [season] programme, and we will confirm a date once their funding is confirmed."]
>
> The programme is a three year collaboration between Anamata Records and the Japanese artist [name],
> building a New Zealand owned Māori presence in the Japanese market around eight waiata, two already
> released.
>
> [Optional, only if true: "We can also offer [marketing support, press introductions, profile in our
> programme, use of our rehearsal space, an artist fee of ¥X]."]
>
> We are happy to be contacted about this letter.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> [Signature]
> [Name, role, organisation]
> [Email] [Phone]

## Template B: studio or recording facility

> [Letterhead]
>
> [Date]
>
> **Estimate and availability: Anamata Records recordings, [year]**
>
> To whom it may concern,
>
> This confirms that [studio name] in [city] is available to [Anamata Records / the collaborating artists] for
> the recording of [number] waiata, totalling approximately [number] days in [month or quarter].
>
> **Estimate**
>
> | Item | Detail | Amount (JPY) |
> |---|---|---|
> | Studio hire | [hours or days, room] | |
> | House engineer | [days] | |
> | Backline and instrument hire | | |
> | [Other] | | |
> | **Total** | [exclusive or inclusive of consumption tax] | |
>
> [Optional: "We have worked with [partner name] on [previous project] and would welcome this project." ]
>
> Please contact me if the dates need to move.
>
> [Signature]
> [Name, role]
> [Address, phone, email]
> [Corporate number (法人番号) and invoice registration number, if the studio has one]

**A quote on studio letterhead is evidence on its own.** It shows the work is costed, which is what the
budget needs, and it shows a third party engaged. Japanese studios issue estimates (お見積書) as a matter of
course, so this is a normal request rather than an unusual one.

## Template C: label, distributor or publisher

> [Letterhead]
>
> [Date]
>
> **Letter of support and terms in principle: Anamata Records Japan programme**
>
> To whom it may concern,
>
> I am [name] of [label or publisher]. We [what we do: release records, distribute, administer publishing].
>
> We confirm our interest in the Anamata Records Japan programme, and our understanding of the rights
> position: masters, compositions and trade marks remain owned in Aotearoa New Zealand, and our involvement is
> a licence or a service for the Japan territory, not a share of ownership in the catalogue.
>
> [State what is offered, in plain terms: "We would release [work] in Japan under a licence for the Japan
> territory, with a term of [years]." Or: "We would administer the works in Japan through [JASRAC /
> NexTone], collecting on behalf of the New Zealand owner and remitting under the current terms."]
>
> [If a fee or a share is proposed, state it: "Our standard terms are [x] per cent of Japan territory
> revenue."]
>
> [Signature]
> [Name, role, organisation, contact details]

If a Japanese label or publisher appears in the application, this letter is the difference between a claim
about the market and evidence of one. The ownership sentence matters: a partner who states in writing that
they are **not** taking a share of the masters is the strongest possible support for the structure the
application promises.

## How many letters, and which ones

An assessor weighs **specificity and independence**, not volume. Three letters with dates and roles beat eight
with warm sentences.

| Priority | From | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A presenter, venue or festival, with a date or a held slot | International demand in the fund's own language: an invitation |
| 2 | The collaborating artist, with the commitments in the invitation letter | The partnership is real and stated |
| 3 | A label, distributor or publisher, with terms in principle | The market route exists, and the ownership position holds |
| 4 | A studio or facility, with a quote and dates | The work is costed and scheduled |
| 5 | An arts body or authority | The sector recognises the programme |

If only two arrive in time, take 1 and 2. If a studio letter is the only one available this week, send it
anyway: a quote with dates is still independent evidence, and the itemised budget it produces is needed
regardless.

## Things that are normal in Japan, and worth knowing

- **Estimates (お見積書)** and **invoices (請求書)** are standard documents of record. Asking for an estimate on
  letterhead is an ordinary business request and it doubles as evidence.
- **A memorandum of understanding (覚書)** is the customary light agreement, shorter and less formal than a
  contract, and easy for a partner to sign. If a full contract is too slow before the deadline, a signed
  memorandum covering the same points is accepted practice and still credible.
- **A registered seal (実印) and its certificate** matter if an entity seals a contract. If the counterparty is
  an individual who signs with a signature, that is valid and no seal certificate is required.
- **Consumption tax registration.** A counterparty who is not registered for Japanese consumption tax cannot
  issue a qualified invoice, which matters to a corporate payer. It does not stop them being a partner or
  invoicing; it only changes how the tax is handled. Ask, and note the answer in the budget notes.
