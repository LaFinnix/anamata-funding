## Form field 3: A summary of your international experience and goals

### Who we are

Anamata Records is the music division of Anamata Kāhui Limited, a Māori led label based in Aotearoa New Zealand, driving the Māori futurism movement in Aotearoa's music industry. The label was founded by Ngāika Smith, a composer, producer and researcher with whakapapa to Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu and Ngāti Awa, supported by a kaitiaki rōpū governance group. Our waiata are composed in te reo Māori, arranged around taonga pūoro and contemporary production, and reviewed for cultural integrity by Precious Clark of Maurea, who has worked with us since 2024.

### International experience

Our international practice has been built artist to artist. In 2025 and 2026 we released two waiata with the Japanese artist KUROKORU, *Not over yet* and *One Voice*, each carrying te reo Māori and Japanese in the same recording rather than one language translated into the other. Releasing those works taught us the real cost of international collaboration for a small Indigenous language label: coordinating across time zones, splitting rights across two jurisdictions, and holding two sets of cultural protocols inside one studio session without either becoming decoration.

The catalogue is grounded in Ngāti Kahungunu kōrero tuku iho and performs to international platforms through Apple Music, YouTube and Spotify. What it does not yet have is a structured international programme with a market, a presentation pathway and measurable targets. That is the gap this application addresses.

### The goal

To establish Anamata Records as a durable Māori presence in the Japanese music market, built on genuinely bilingual waiata and on cross Indigenous creative protocols that both parties can defend. Japan is the market for three reasons: an existing creative relationship, a market with a proven appetite for Indigenous language music, and a listening culture that rewards craft led records over trend led ones. Our third collaboration is in progress.

### The three year programme

**Year one, 2027: Build and record.** Establish the collaboration formally with a written protocol agreement covering language use, cultural review, rights division and mana over the work. Compose and record a bilingual EP of four waiata, te reo Māori and Japanese, with taonga pūoro in a lead role. Register the works with APRA and the Japanese equivalent. Deliver a written international strategy.

**Year two, 2028: Present.** Take the programme to the Japanese market in person: two performances for Japanese industry in Tokyo and one regional centre, industry meetings with Japanese labels, publishers and festival programmers, and a co writing residency in Aotearoa hosting our Japanese collaborators on Kahungunu and Ngāi Tahu whenua. Release two further waiata.

**Year three, 2029: Consolidate.** Release a full length collaborative album. Present at a major international festival. Publish the cross Indigenous creative protocol framework openly so other Māori artists can reuse it, and hand the model to a second Māori act so the pathway outlives this grant.

### What the investment changes

Three things. It converts an informal and lucky international relationship into a documented and repeatable pathway. It is the difference between releasing waiata into Japan and building an audience there. And it puts the method, not just the music, into the public domain, so that the next Māori label does not have to learn cross Indigenous collaboration through the same expensive trial and error that we did.
