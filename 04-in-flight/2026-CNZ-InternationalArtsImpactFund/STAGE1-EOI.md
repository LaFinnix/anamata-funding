## Field 3: A summary of your international experience and goals

### Who we are

Anamata Records is the music division of Anamata Kāhui Limited, part of Te Kāhui Anamata, a Māori led label in Aotearoa New Zealand driving the Māori futurism movement in Aotearoa's music industry. The label was founded by Ngaika Smith, a composer, producer and researcher with whakapapa to Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu and Ngāti Awa, and is governed with a kaitiaki rōpū. Our waiata are composed in te reo Māori, arranged around taonga pūoro and contemporary production, and reviewed for cultural integrity before release.

### What we have built internationally

Our international work started with a single collaboration and has grown into a partnership. Working with the Japanese artist Ryo Suzuki, we have released two waiata that hold te reo Māori and Japanese as equal voices in one recording rather than as a translation of one another: まだ終わっちゃいない (Not over yet) and ただ声一つ (One Voice). A third collaborative work is in production.

Making those records taught us what international collaboration actually costs a small Indigenous language label. Coordinating across time zones. Splitting rights across two jurisdictions. Holding two sets of cultural protocols inside one studio session so that neither becomes decoration. We learned it by trial, at our own expense, and we would rather the next Māori label did not have to.

### What we are building in Japan

Te Kāhui Anamata is establishing a Japanese market presence through Anamata Records. No Japanese entity has been established yet. The structure is being worked through now, deliberately, and the design is that ownership and control stay in Aotearoa New Zealand. I am in Japan as this Expression of Interest is filed, working through the formation of the vehicle and the partnership terms that will sit around it.

### Structure, rights and ownership

The vehicle will be a single member gōdō gaisha (合同会社), the Japanese limited liability company, held 100 percent by Anamata Kāhui Limited. Japan permits full foreign ownership of a gōdō gaisha, and recorded music is not a restricted sector, so a New Zealand company can hold the entire entity with no Japanese co-member and no local shareholder requirement. It is also the lighter vehicle: one member, an executive manager appointed by that member, and articles of incorporation that can require the member's consent for any transfer of interest, which locks ownership in place.

The rights stay here. The Japanese entity will hold no master rights and no publishing. Master recordings and compositions remain owned in Aotearoa New Zealand by Anamata Kāhui Limited, or by the writers and producers with an exclusive licence to it. The Japanese entity operates under an exclusive licence for the Japan territory: it books, markets, releases into the Japanese market and administers local collection, and it remits licence fees back to New Zealand. Ownership of the catalogue therefore never leaves the country, and if the Japanese operation ever stopped trading, the catalogue would not be inside it.

Ryo Suzuki is our collaborating artist and a partner to the organisation, and that partnership is contractual rather than equity: a defined share of Japan territory revenue and a named creative role. Market access, introductions, studio relationships, translation and language work can all be contracted and paid for honestly. A joint venture would hand over a permanent share of the masters in exchange for services that are worth a fee, which is why we are not proposing one.

Still being worked through: the registered address and resident representative, the corporate bank account, the articles of incorporation, and confirmation of the royalty position under the New Zealand and Japan double tax agreement with our accountant before the first licence fee is invoiced.

Each has its own focus, so that every waiata can stand as its own release, reach its own audience, and carry its own future production application: an NZ On Air New Music Single in most cases, or Waiata Takitahi where the waiata is te reo Māori led. The album is funded song by song rather than as one lump.

### The three year programme

**Year one, 2027: Build and record.** Establish the Japanese vehicle and formalise the partnership into written agreements covering language use, cultural review, rights division and mana over the work. Compose and record four waiata from the slate: *Kotahi te kaupapa*, *Kua muia e te pouritanga*, *Te Ara Moana* and *Tapuwae tipuna*. Release *Kotahi te kaupapa* into the Japanese market as the launch single with a bilingual campaign. Register the works with APRA and their Japanese counterparts. Publish the international strategy as a standing document.

**Year two, 2028: Present.** Release *Rua Reo* and *Mokemoke*. Take the programme into the Japanese market in person: two performances for Japanese industry in Tokyo and one regional centre, six or more meetings with Japanese labels, publishers and festival programmers, and a co-writing residency in Aotearoa hosting Ryo Suzuki and the wider partner network on Kahungunu and Ngāi Tahu whenua.

**Year three, 2029: Consolidate.** Release the full length collaborative album gathering the slate. Present at a major international festival. Publish the cross Indigenous creative protocol framework openly and hand the model to a second Māori act, so the pathway outlives this grant and stops depending on us.

### What this investment changes

It turns a relationship built on instinct into an institution we own, so the collaboration survives a change of personnel on either side. It moves us from releasing waiata into Japan to holding the structure that reaches Japanese audiences, which is the difference between visiting a market and being in one. And it publishes the method, so the next Māori label enters an international market with a protocol and an ownership position rather than a guess.
