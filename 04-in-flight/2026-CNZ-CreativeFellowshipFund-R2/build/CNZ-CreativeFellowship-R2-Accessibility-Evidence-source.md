# Accessibility programme evidence

## Supplementary evidence for the Creative Fellowship Fund 2026, Round 2 application

Compiled 21 September 2026. Every link in this document was fetched and returned a live page on that date.
This annex is written for an assessor who wants to check the application's accessibility claims rather than
take them on trust.

## What this document is

The fellowship application says the accessibility infrastructure underneath the music is being built
through a funded programme, and that the fellowship is the artistic research that goes further than that
programme can. This document sets out what that programme is, what can be verified publicly today, what is
still ongoing, and what does not exist yet. The last section is the important one for an assessor,
because it states plainly what this fellowship would create that nothing else is creating.

## How the two programmes relate

They interlock on dates and they do not overlap on activity.

| | Development Fund programme | This fellowship |
|---|---|---|
| Applicant | Anamata Kāhui Limited | Ngaika Smith as an individual |
| Nature | Organisational capability: audit, training, standards, policy | Artistic research: tactile-first composition, notation, co-composed waiata |
| Period | July 2026 to June 2027 | December 2026 to December 2027 |
| Output | An accessibility standard, a training record, a published plan and charter | A composition method, a notation system, six waiata with tactile scores, an open protocol |

The vibrotactile production trial in December 2026 and the mix protocol prototype in February 2027 sit
inside the first phase of the fellowship. The fellowship uses them as a starting rig and a starting
protocol, and then goes where a capability programme is not designed to go: into how the music itself is
written, what a tactile score is, and what happens when a waiata is composed for the body first.

## What does not exist yet

Stated plainly, because the fellowship is the answer to it.

- No tactile score exists for any waiata, in this catalogue or anywhere else in Aotearoa that I can find.
- No waiata has been composed tactile-first.
- No Deaf artist, NZSL poet or tāngata whaikaha Māori performer has yet been engaged to co-compose.
- The vibrotactile mix protocol exists as a planned prototype, not as a published standard.
- NZSL and Easy Read production for the platform is committed with target dates, not delivered.
