# People and organisations involved in the activity

## Participation record, Creative Fellowship Fund 2026 Round 2

This document sets out the people and organisations involved in the proposed activity, and the role each
one plays. It mirrors the participation records entered in the portal.

## 1. Ngaika Smith

Composer and producer, applying as an individual. Whakapapa to Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu and Ngāti Awa.
Leads Te Kāhui Anamata, trading as Anamata Records, where the released catalogue was written and produced
including Te Tinihanga, Wairua Piri Ta'i and Te Whakamātautau, and the bilingual collaborations Not over yet
and One Voice. Recipient of a 2026 Creative New Zealand Organizations and Groups Development Fund grant of
$10,000 for a programme of bicultural capability and universal accessibility, and project manager and lead
researcher on that programme. Fellowship role: lead artist, researcher and composer.

## 2. Anamata Records, a trading name of Anamata Kāhui Limited

Māori-led music label and cultural platform. Provides the studio and post-production infrastructure the
fellowship's recordings are made in, the trilingual release workflow in English, te reo Māori and New
Zealand Sign Language, the cultural review process that sits at draft stage on every release, and kaitiaki
rōpū governance over the programme. Role: production infrastructure, cultural governance, and release of the
three recorded works.

## 3. Maurea, Precious Clark

Māori cultural consultant, contracted through Maurea for cultural review at draft stage on every waiata, and
for review of the research protocol itself before any session runs. Works in Te Tiriti-led governance and
runs the Te Kaa and Te Tiriti programmes. Also named as the cultural consultant for the 2026 Creative New
Zealand Development Fund programme, where the relationship is being formalised. Role: cultural review,
guidance on te reo terminology in the tactile notation, programme-scope oversight.

## 4. Arts Access Aotearoa, Stace Robertson

National organisation for access to the arts, running the Arts For All regional networks and the Deaf and
disabled artist fellowship. Stace Robertson leads that framework and is named Lead Accessibility Advisor in
the 2026 Development Fund programme, with engagement under way. Role: accessibility and haptic advice at
phase reviews, help reaching Deaf and tāngata whaikaha Māori artists for the co-composition wānanga, and
distribution of the published method through the accessibility networks.

## 5. Access Advisors, Dr Chandra Harrison

Digital accessibility consultancy, named in the 2026 Development Fund programme for the digital
accessibility audit and vibrotactile production work, with partner onboarding under way. Role: advisory
input on the tactile test programme, review of the phase one figures alongside the collaborators, and
comment on how measured results are documented.

## 6. WordsWorth Interpreting

New Zealand Sign Language interpreting agency, and the named interpretation partner in the 2026 Development
Fund programme. Qualified interpreters and, through their networks, Deaf practitioners and NZSL poets.
Role: two qualified interpreters per collaborative studio wānanga, preparation time included, plus
interpretation for the two public wānanga.

## 7. Deaf artists, NZSL poets and tāngata whaikaha Māori performers (to be confirmed)

Paid co-composers, selected within 30 days of award through networks already in place, including Arts Access
Aotearoa's Arts For All networks, WordsWorth Interpreting, and Deaf arts companies. None has been
approached for this project, and none is being asked to contribute unpaid time before funding exists. Each
will be contracted at a fair pay day rate, credited on the work, and involved in the tactile test programme,
the measurement of thresholds on bodies other than the composer's, and the composition of the six waiata. At
least one of the three mentoring places is held for a Deaf composer.

## 8. Taonga pūoro mentor (to be selected with the cultural reviewer)

Practitioner in taonga pūoro, selected with the cultural reviewer from Ngāti Kahungunu, Ngāi Tahu and
Ngāti Awa networks after award. Role: supervised instrument practice and guidance on how traditional
instruments sit inside a composition where the primary version is felt, contracted and paid for their time.

## 9. SoundCheck Aotearoa and the Māori Music Industry Collective, Wairere Iti

Music industry bodies working on sector standards and support, including the Safer Spaces programme.
Wairere Iti is named as industry advisor in the 2026 Development Fund programme. Role: presentation of the
method and the open protocol to the sector, and distribution through their networks so other composers can
use it.
