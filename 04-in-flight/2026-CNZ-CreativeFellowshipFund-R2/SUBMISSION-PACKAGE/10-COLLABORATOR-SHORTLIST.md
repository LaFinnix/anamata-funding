# Collaborator shortlist: Deaf artists and tāngata whaikaha Māori

Compiled 21 September 2026 for the somatosensory fellowship. Every name and role below was read off a live
page on that date, with the source given. Where something could not be verified, it says so rather than
filling the gap.

Search engines were unusable from this machine (Bing, DuckDuckGo and Mojeek all refused or returned empty
results), so this is built from sites fetched directly. It is a starting list for introductions, not a
complete map of the sector.

## Tier 1: approach first

### Equal Voices Arts
Aotearoa's first professional touring Deaf and hearing theatre company, based in Kirikiriroa Hamilton and
working nationally. Performs in New Zealand Sign Language and spoken English, devises collaboratively with
"no hierarchy of language, culture or creative input", and runs training and workshops for Deaf artists.
They received a Ministry for Culture and Heritage Innovation Fund grant for a two year pilot programme
designed for D/deaf artists and to help arts organisations serve Deaf audiences better.

Source: equalvoicesarts.com/about/the-company/ and the Arts Access Aotearoa article at
artsaccess.org.nz/equal-voices-arts-to-lead-pilot-programme

Named people, from equalvoicesarts.com/about/the-people/:

| Person | Role | Why they matter here |
|---|---|---|
| Rāhera (Rachel) Turner | Company manager, NZSL creative consultant | Deaf arts leadership plus NZSL consultancy. Named in the Innovation Fund article on ensuring Deaf leadership in the arts. The natural first conversation. |
| Dr Laura Haughey | Artistic director | Company lead and the person who would sign off a collaboration. |
| Andy Duggan | Musical director, composer, filmmaker | The music side. A Deaf-company composer is the most useful collaborator for a composition fellowship, and the most useful person to test the tactile method against. |
| Denise Armstrong | Associate artist, research associate | Described in the Arts Access article as a Deaf sign theatre expert, co-leading workshops. Research-plus-practice fit. |
| Cian Parker, Shaun Fahey, Thora Hübner, Mihailo Ladevac | Performers, devisors, workshop facilitators | The performer pool for co-composition and for the technical tests with real performers rather than test signals. |

Approach route: their site carries a contact form only, no published email. A form enquiry addressed to
Rāhera Turner and Dr Laura Haughey, or an introduction through Arts Access Aotearoa.

### Charlotte Nightingale
Recipient of the 2022 Whakahoa Kaitoi Te Puna Toi Creative New Zealand Arts For All Fellowship, based in
Warkworth. Her fellowship project worked with the profound and multiple learning disabilities community to
create "Spark", a multisensory theatre work for audiences with PMLD.

Source: artsaccess.org.nz/recipients-of-fellowships-2022

Why she matters: multisensory work for audiences who cannot rely on hearing is the closest existing
practice to this fellowship in Aotearoa. She is also proof that this territory is funded here.

Contact route not verified. Ask Stace Robertson at Arts Access Aotearoa, who runs the Arts For All
Fellowship, for a warm introduction.

## What I could not verify

- **No individual tāngata whaikaha Māori performer or Māori Deaf artist with a public profile could be
  confirmed** from this machine. The language exists in the sector ("tāngata turi and tāngata whaikaha
  Māori" appears in Arts Access Aotearoa's own fellowship criteria), but the named individuals are not
  surfacing through the sites I could reach. Rāhera Turner at Equal Voices Arts is the strongest
  individual lead, on the strength of her role and name rather than a verified iwi affiliation.
- Contact emails for Equal Voices Arts, Charlotte Nightingale and the individual artists are not published
  on the pages I could reach.
- No verification of who is currently available, willing or funded elsewhere.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Stace Robertson, Arts Access Aotearoa** in the same email as the advisor conversation. Ask for
   introductions and for the Arts For All network contacts in Te Whanganui-a-Tara and Te Matau-a-Māui,
   which are the two places the fellowship plans wānanga.
2. **Rāhera Turner and Dr Laura Haughey, Equal Voices Arts**, once Stace has either introduced or
   confirmed the company is the right first call.
3. **Deaf Aotearoa**, for Māori Deaf networks and for a cultural check on how the project describes Deaf
   leadership.
4. **Charlotte Nightingale**, through Arts Access, for the multisensory practice.

## What this changes in the application

Nothing yet. The application discloses honestly that no collaborator has been approached and that
selection happens within 30 days of award. If any of these approaches land before Thursday, the name goes
into Question 1 and the letter of support list, and the application gets materially stronger. If they land
after submission, they strengthen the project regardless.
