1. NAME
Ngaika Smith

ABOUT
Composer and producer, applying as an individual. Whakapapa to Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu and Ngāti Awa.
Leads Te Kāhui Anamata, trading as Anamata Records, where the released catalogue was written and produced
including Te Tinihanga, Wairua Piri Ta'i and Te Whakamātautau, and the bilingual collaborations Not over yet
and One Voice. Recipient of a 2026 Creative New Zealand Organizations and Groups Development Fund grant of
$10,000 for a programme of bicultural capability and universal accessibility. Project manager and lead
researcher on that programme. Fellowship role: lead artist, researcher and composer.


2. NAME
Anamata Records, a trading name of Anamata Kāhui Limited

ABOUT
Māori-led music label and cultural platform. Provides the studio and post-production infrastructure the
fellowship's recordings are made in, along with the trilingual release workflow in English, te reo Māori and
New Zealand Sign Language, the cultural review process that sits at draft stage on every release, and
kaitiaki rōpū governance over the programme. Whānau and hapū relationships that the waiata draw on are held
here. Role in this project: production infrastructure, cultural governance, and release of the three
recorded works.


3. NAME
Maurea, Precious Clark

ABOUT
Māori cultural consultant, contracted through Maurea for cultural review at draft stage on every waiata in
the fellowship, and for review of the research protocol itself before any session runs. Works in
Te Tiriti-led governance and runs the Te Kaa and Te Tiriti programmes. Also named as the cultural
consultant for the 2026 Creative New Zealand Development Fund programme, where the relationship is being
formalised. Role in this project: cultural review, guidance on te reo terminology in the tactile notation,
and programme-scope oversight.


4. NAME
Arts Access Aotearoa, Stace Robertson

ABOUT
National organisation for access to the arts, and the body that runs the Arts For All regional networks and
the Deaf and disabled artist fellowship. Stace Robertson leads that framework and is named as Lead
Accessibility Advisor in the 2026 Creative New Zealand Development Fund programme, with engagement under
way. Role in this project: accessibility and haptic advice at phase reviews, help reaching Deaf and
tāngata whaikaha Māori artists for the co-composition wānanga, and distribution of the published method
through the accessibility networks.


5. NAME
Access Advisors, Dr Chandra Harrison

ABOUT
Digital accessibility consultancy. Named in the 2026 Creative New Zealand Development Fund programme for
the digital accessibility audit and the vibrotactile production work, with partner onboarding under way
under that programme. Role in this project: advisory input on the tactile test programme, review of the
phase one figures alongside the collaborators, and comment on how the measured results are documented.


6. NAME
WordsWorth Interpreting

ABOUT
New Zealand Sign Language interpreting agency, and the named interpretation partner in the 2026 Creative
New Zealand Development Fund programme. Qualified interpreters and, through their networks, Deaf
practitioners and NZSL poets. Role in this project: two qualified interpreters per collaborative studio
wānanga, preparation time included, and interpretation for the two public wānanga. Engagement under way
under the 2026 programme.


7. NAME
Deaf artists, NZSL poets and tāngata whaikaha Māori performers (to be confirmed)

ABOUT
Paid co-composers, selected within 30 days of award through networks already in place, including Arts
Access Aotearoa's Arts For All networks, WordsWorth Interpreting, and Deaf arts companies. None has been
approached for this project, and none is being asked to contribute unpaid time before funding exists. Each
will be contracted at a fair pay day rate, credited on the work, and involved in the tactile test programme,
the measurement of thresholds on bodies other than the composer's, and the composition of the six waiata.
At least one of the three mentoring places is held for a Deaf composer.


8. NAME
Taonga pūoro mentor (to be selected with the cultural reviewer)

ABOUT
Practitioner in taonga pūoro, selected with the cultural reviewer from Ngāti Kahungunu, Ngāi Tahu and
Ngāti Awa networks after award. Role in this project: supervised instrument practice and guidance on how
traditional instruments sit inside a composition where the primary version is felt, contracted and paid for
their time.


9. NAME
SoundCheck Aotearoa and the Māori Music Industry Collective, Wairere Iti

ABOUT
Music industry bodies working on sector standards and support, including the Safer Spaces programme.
Wairere Iti is named as industry advisor in the 2026 Creative New Zealand Development Fund programme.
Role in this project: presentation of the method and the open protocol to the sector, and distribution of
it through their networks so other composers can use it.
