SECTION 2 — THE INITIATIVE — GOALS + AIMS
================================================================================

[ ~ 600 words — the online form's primary narrative field ]

The initiative operationalises the Te Mana Raraunga CARE principles
for Indigenous data governance at the schema layer of a digital
platform, then publishes the three underlying methodologies as
citable research artefacts so other iwi / hapū / GLAM organisations
can adopt them without re-inventing the wheel.

The platform itself — Anamata Kāhui — has been built and is live at
anamatakahui.co.nz. It is one of very few Aotearoa digital platforms
where cultural safety is enforced by the database rather than as a
procedural convention: a Postgres BEFORE UPDATE trigger ensures no
release goes out without kaitiaki (cultural guardian) sign-off, the
append-only consent log preserves every consent decision (granted,
amended, withdrawn, expired) with named actors and timestamps, and
every iwi-gate decision is queryable for audit.

The methodology papers are already published at version 1.0 on Zenodo
with permanent DOIs (see Section 5 for the list). The aim of this
initiative is to bring them to publication-grade for academic and
funder consumption through four specific deliverables:

  1. INDEPENDENT PEER REVIEW of all three methodology papers by a
     Māori data sovereignty academic. The papers are version 1.0
     drafts; peer review converts them into v2.0 with reviewer
     notes credited and revisions incorporated.

  2. A CARE CONFORMANCE BENCHMARK report on the first 3-5 consenting
     NZ organisations (iwi trusts, GLAM institutions, research
     bodies). Run the CARE Auditor on each, publish an aggregate
     "state of NZ Māori data governance 2026" report, and demonstrate
     the methodology in real use rather than in the abstract.

  3. DOI MINTING of the three methodology papers via Zenodo (CERN),
     so each paper becomes a citable research output with a
     permanent identifier that survives repository moves, branch
     deletions, or platform shutdown.

  4. TE REO MĀORI UPGRADE of the CARE Auditor and Provenance Embedder
     interfaces, plus native review of every translation string by a
     te reo Māori translator, plus re-recording of the methodology
     paper abstracts in te reo Māori + English side-by-side.

The platform already ships the *infrastructure* (CARE Auditor + Provenance
Embedder live on the website, kaitiaki rōpū governance, three methodology
papers on GitHub, two koha reciprocity views, the iwi-gate consent log).
What this initiative adds is the *publication-grade evidence surface*
that makes the infrastructure useful beyond our own iwi — citable for
other researchers, evaluable for funders, replicable by other iwi.

The specific Tindall fit — under "Identity and social cohesion" — is the
"Cultural data sovereignty infrastructure" deliverable plus the peer
review deliverable. Tindall's giving priorities explicitly name
Mātauranga Māori, lived-experience voices, and place-based leadership;
our methodology papers operationalise all three, with named-actor
audit trails and kaitiaki rōpū governance that is structurally
embedded rather than performative.

SECTION 4 — OUTCOMES / IMPACT
================================================================================

Outcome 1 — Citable research artefacts (3 DOIs)

Each of the three methodology papers becomes a permanent citable
output that survives the platform's lifecycle. Any iwi, hapū, GLAM
institution, research body, or policy actor can cite the papers in
their own work, and the methodology becomes part of the international
conversation on Indigenous data sovereignty rather than a one-off
blog post.

  - DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21693726 — Cultural Review as a Database-
    Enforced Workflow (181 lines, v1.0 published 2026-07-29)
  - DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21693730 — Operationalising CARE Principles
    in a Web Platform (175 lines, v1.0 published 2026-07-29)
  - DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21693732 — Both-Party Attestation as a
    First-Class Data Primitive (211 lines, v1.0 published 2026-07-29)

Outcome 2 — Conformance evidence in real NZ organisations

The CARE benchmark report demonstrates the methodology's practical
utility. Even with 3-5 organisations, the report generates
publishable evidence about which CARE principles are well-implemented
across the sector and which are gaps. This informs both the platform's
own iteration and the wider Māori data sovereignty community.

Outcome 3 — Replicable infrastructure for other iwi

The CARE Auditor + Provenance Embedder are both free, public, and
operate without requiring a Local Contexts Hub account. Any iwi can
audit their own data governance against the CARE principles and
generate the gap-analysis + recommended Local Contexts labels
without any platform-specific knowledge. The Provenance Embedder
similarly lets any GLAM institution attach Local Contexts Hub labels
to their digital assets without developer resources.

Outcome 4 — Te reo Māori platform fluency

The te reo upgrade ensures that the CARE Auditor and Provenance
Embedder are genuinely usable in te reo Māori, not just labelled in
te reo Māori. Native review by a te reo Māori translator + re-recording
of the methodology abstracts in bilingual side-by-side format means
iwi practitioners can engage with the platform in their first
language. This is operational, not symbolic.

Outcome 5 — Operational governance transparency

The kaitiaki rōpū governance model + iwi-gate consent log + named-
actor accountability rows in the cultural_review_cycles table
generate a continuous audit trail that is queryable for compliance,
funding verification, and community accountability. The
/transparency page on the platform surfaces this in real time.

Outcome 6 — Multi-funder compatible evidence surface

A single Tindall investment serves subsequent rounds of funding
applications across other NZ cultural funders (NZ On Air, Creative
NZ, HRC, Royal Society Te Apārangi). The CARE Auditor + Provenance
Embedder + methodology papers satisfy multiple funders' criteria
simultaneously because the operational evidence surface is structured,
not improvised.

Outcome 7 — Long-term platform durability

The DOIs make the methodology papers independent of the platform's
operational continuity. Even if Anamata Kāhui Limited ceased operations,
the papers would still be citable research outputs. This is the
appropriate durability for an infrastructure investment.


SECTION 5 — LOCATION + REACH
================================================================================

Primary reach: NATIONAL.

The platform itself is hosted in Aotearoa (Vercel edge network + Supabase
Auckland region), the kaitiaki rōpū governance is Aotearoa-based, and
the methodology papers are written for Aotearoa context (Te Mana
Raraunga, Te Tiriti o Waitangi, NZ Companies Office, NZ Public Records
Act).

Secondary reach: INTERNATIONAL (academic citation network).

The methodology papers are written in a way that travels beyond Aotearoa
context. The CARE principles have international application; the
operational pattern (cultural safety as a database-enforced workflow)
is generalisable to any Indigenous data sovereignty context; the
Local Contexts Hub integration is international infrastructure. The
DOIs (10.5281/zenodo.21693726, 10.5281/zenodo.21693730, 10.5281/zenodo.21693732)
are DataCite-minted and indexed internationally.

Specific locations of mahi:
  - Auckland (Vercel + Supabase hosting region)
  - Wellington (kaitiaki rōpū + governance body location)
  - Christchurch (founder + cultural reviewer engagement)
  - Whakatāne / Ngāti Awa region (multi-iwi context — founder has
    whakapapa connections here; benchmark organisations likely to
    include at least 1 from Te Moana-a-Toi)
  - Anywhere with internet access (the public tools are available
    globally; the methodology papers are open access)

Tindall's 3 priority regions (Te Tai Tokerau, Tairāwhiti, Horowhenua &
Whanganui) are not directly in our operational footprint. The
applicant's national reach + academic citation reach are the
operational evidence surface for this initiative, not direct service
delivery in those 3 regions. If Tindall's priority is regional
footprint specifically, this initiative is a better fit for the
"Identity and social cohesion" focus area than for the regional
priority — the methodology papers benefit Māori data sovereignty
nationally and internationally rather than serving a specific
geographic community.
