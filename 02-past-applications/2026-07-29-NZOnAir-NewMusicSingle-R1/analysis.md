| Cultural-review evidence | Generic kaitiaki-rōpū governance | Specific cultural-review cycle #CR-2026-04 named in application |

The biggest risk: the **3 named roles TBC** could be flagged by an assessor as "incomplete application." Honest disclosure is the right call (per the platform's own methodology — see `docs/METHODOLOGY-CULTURAL-REVIEW.md` §5 "Stress Tests" — we're meant to ship what we can verify) but the application should be reviewed by Fin before submission to decide whether to:

- **Option A:** Submit as-is, with the honest disclosure. The TBC items are all named candidates from a partner network; selection happens within 7–14 days of award, which is normal in NZ On Air production grants.
- **Option B:** Wait until director + interpreter + distributor are formally confirmed, then submit. Cost: probably misses Round 1's 13 Aug deadline.
- **Option C:** Switch the application to **Waiata Takitahi (Round 2, opens 15 Oct)** which has a longer runway to confirm the production team. Cost: $15k instead of $11k, and the waiata subject would change.

**My recommendation:** Option A — submit as-is, with the honest TBC disclosure. NZ On Air assessors see production grants every week; "named candidates from partner network, formalising post-award" is a routine pattern. The waiata subject (Te Awa o Rangatira) and the cultural-review evidence are the strongest parts of the application; don't lose the window waiting on a distributor shortlist.
