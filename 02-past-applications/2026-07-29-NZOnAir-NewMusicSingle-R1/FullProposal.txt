Executive Summary

Anamata Records is a Māori-led music label operating at the intersection of
contemporary sound and traditional indigenous knowledge. We are requesting
NZD $11,000 from NZ On Air's New Music Single Round 1 2026 to support the
creation, recording, release, and promotion of "Te Awa o Rangatira" — a
bilingual (te reo Māori + English) single anchored in the cultural kōrero
of our founder's whakapapa to Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu, and Ngāti Awa.

The waiata is the second single from the Anamata Records 2026 release cycle,
which already includes five released waiata across Apple Music, Spotify,
and YouTube. Our first four waiata were produced on internal capital and a
modest Ngāi Tahu Puna Pakihi Business Grant; this round represents Anamata's
first application to NZ On Air and our entry point into the funded release
track.

What distinguishes this waiata from a generic music release is the cultural
infrastructure underneath it. The track is being recorded, released, and
documented on the Anamata Kāhui platform (anamatakahui.co.nz) where every
release carries a Cultural Provenance Hero: the Local Contexts Hub label
suite (TK Attribution + BC Provenance + Caring) is applied to each waiata,
each release has a published kaitiaki-review cycle record, and the
acknowledged iwi affiliations are surfaced as primary metadata. Anamata
Records is one of two Aotearoa music platforms where cultural review is a
database-enforced workflow gate — a release cannot transition from draft
to scheduled status without a published kaitiaki rōpū approval — and the
waiata's evidence trail will be visible to any funder or audience member
who visits the track's page on the platform.

The waiata will be released across Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, and
iwi-radio broadcast (Waatea, Te Hiku o Te Ika). The accompanying music
video (4-minute cinematic, filmed across Te Waipounamu + Te Ika-a-Māui
locations) will carry embedded captions in English, te reo Māori, and an
NZSL on-screen interpreter for the chorus and narrative sections — closing
the 2026 Creative NZ-winning application's trilingual commitment. The total
project budget of $11,000 is allocated across studio recording, music video
production, trilingual captioning, distribution, and a 5% contingency.

Strategic Context

The evolution of the music industry in Aotearoa New Zealand has reached
a critical juncture where artistic excellence is inseparable from
cultural accountability and infrastructure. Music is no longer measured
by audio fidelity alone — funders, broadcasters, and audiences increasingly
evaluate a release by the rigour of its cultural review trail, the
attestation of its iwi affiliations, and the openness of its provenance
metadata. Anamata Records bridges the gap between contemporary Aotearoa
music and traditional indigenous knowledge by treating cultural safety as
infrastructure, not as a checkbox.

Anamata Kāhui Limited is the parent company of Anamata Records. The
broader Anamata Kāhui ecosystem operates four branches (Anamata Records,
Research & Language Preservation, Creative Arts, Technology & Development)
under a tikanga Māori operating model. The platform itself is open-source
and serves as both the release surface for our waiata and a public evidence
ledger for the cultural-review, data-sovereignty, and reciprocity
methodologies we apply.

The New Music Single Round 1 2026 window is the first of two NZ On Air
music rounds this year, paired with Waiata Takitahi (the bilingual single
co-funded with Te Māngai Pāho, opening 15 October). This particular
single is positioned for Round 1 because the recording is further advanced
than the rest of the catalogue — the waiata is composed, the studio is
booked, and the cultural review is mid-cycle — whereas the Waiata
Takitahi-bound waiata is still in pre-production. A Round 1 submission
gives the waiata a release window in Q4 2026, with the Round 2 / Waiata
Takitahi track reserved for the November–December window.

QUESTION 1 — What is the song, who is performing it, and why this song now?

Te Awa o Rangatira is a 4-minute bilingual (te reo Māori + English) waiata
performed by Ngāika Smith under the Anamata Records label. The composition
draws on the riverine kōrero of Ngāi Tahu — specifically the Awa-o-Rangatira
whakapapa tracing the Arahura river system back to the founding chiefs —
and braids it with a contemporary Aotearoa pop arrangement (cinematic
strings, taonga puoro, baritone lead over a layered choral refrain).

The narrative centres on a single line — "Ko au te awa, ko te awa ko au" /
"I am the river, the river is me" — and unpacks the relational identity
between tāngata whenua and the water that sustains them. It is a song
about intergenerational belonging, written for the rakatahi who carry
these waters in their blood but have not yet stood on the riverbank.

The song is being recorded now because the cultural-review cycle for the
underlying pūrākau is mid-process (Cultural Review #CR-2026-04 in the
Anamata Kāhui database) and the studio time at Roundhead Studios (Auckland)
is booked for the first week of September. The video shoot is scheduled
for late September at the Arahura river site (Ngāi Tahu rohe) and the
Te Whanganui-a-Tara site (Ngāti Kahungunu rohe), giving the waiata a
dual-iwi visual frame that reflects our founder's whakapapa. The release
target is mid-October, ahead of the Waiata Takitahi window and into the
Christmas release cycle for Aotearoa music consumers.

The cultural review process for Te Awa o Rangatira is led by Precious
Clark / Maurea (engaged cultural reviewer for Anamata Records since 2024),
with an in-conversation kōrero logged in the Anamata Kāhui cultural_review_
cycles table. The release will not proceed to scheduled status until the
kaitiaki rōpū approval is recorded — this is enforced at the database
layer, not as a procedural checkbox, via the require_cultural_signoff()
trigger on the releases table.

QUESTION 3 — Who is the audience and how will they find it?

Primary audience:
  - Māori music listeners in Aotearoa, estimated 60,000+ regular
    consumers of te reo Māori music (RNZ / NZ On Air audience research
    baseline)
  - Rangatahi Māori (ages 16–30) who follow the contemporary Māori
    music scene across Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, and TikTok
  - Te reo Māori speakers and learners seeking culturally-grounded
    music for daily use

Secondary audience:
  - Aotearoa world-music listeners (estimated 250,000+ monthly Spotify
    listeners in the "Aotearoa Indie" + "Māori" combined categories)
  - Pacific music listeners in Aotearoa and the broader Pacific
    (RNZ Pacific audience overlap)
  - Global Māori diaspora — particularly Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu,
    Ngāti Awa communities in Australia, UK, and the Pacific Northwest
  - Cultural-curious listeners discovering Aotearoa music through
    NZ On Air–funded playlists

Audience reach plan:
  - Week 1: Soft launch to cultural reviewer network + iwi-radio
    broadcasters; collect early feedback
  - Week 2: Public release across all platforms; coordinated social
    push via Instagram Reels + TikTok (one-minute lyric-video excerpt
    + NZSL interpreter excerpt)
  - Week 3–4: iwi-radio broadcast windows (Waatea, Te Hiku o Te Ika)
    with on-air interviews about the cultural-review process
  - Week 5–8: Sustained promotion through Anamata Kāhui's existing
    mailing list + partner network

The audience evidence for the broader Anamata Kāhui platform already
exists at anamatakahui.co.nz/impact (live metrics, refresh daily) and
anamata-kahui/credits (the koha reciprocity ledger, which the waiata
will appear in once its release cycle is complete).

Why Anamata Records

Anamata Records is led by Ngāika Smith, a researcher and music producer
with whakapapa to Ngāi Tahu, Ngāti Kahungunu, and Ngāti Awa, with a
dedicated commitment to the Māori futurism movement in Aotearoa's music
sector. Anamata Kāhui Limited (NZBN 9429052960734, company 9350922) is
the parent company. We previously received NZD $10,000 from Creative
New Zealand's 2026 Organizations and Groups Development Fund for a
12-month cultural-competency + accessibility capability programme. Our
cultural reviewer is Precious Clark / Maurea (engaged since 2024).

Our release catalogue currently includes five released waiata, available
on Apple Music, Spotify, and YouTube. Each release carries the Local
Contexts Hub label suite and a published kaitiaki-review record. The
Anamata Kāhui platform at anamatakahui.co.nz is open-source and serves
as the public evidence ledger for our cultural-accountability work —
including the methodology papers for cultural-review-as-database-
enforcement, CARE-principle operationalisation, and koha-attestation
that are citable as research outputs.

(Round 1 2026) represents a commitment to a future where indigenous
