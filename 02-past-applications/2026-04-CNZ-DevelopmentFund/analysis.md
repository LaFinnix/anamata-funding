# Anamata Records — 2026 Creative NZ Development Fund (WON)

**Round:** Creative New Zealand — Organizations and Groups Development Fund
**Year:** 2026
**Amount awarded:** NZD $10,000 (the maximum grant)
**Programme:** 12-month capability-building
**Application date:** 10 April 2026
**Outcome:** ✅ **Successful**

## What the application was actually for

Two-pillar 12-month capability-building programme:

1. **Cultural Competency** — embedding Te Tiriti o Waitangi and te ao
   Māori into Anamata's governance, contracts, policies.
2. **Accessibility** — Deaf and disabled community inclusion,
   including NZSL, vibrotactile/haptic production, universal design.

The deliverables: gap analysis → staff training (NZQA Level 4
Bicultural Competency + NZSL workshops) → 5-year Accessibility Plan +
Accessibility Charter + Māori and Disabled Advisory Board.

## Structure that won (memorise this — it worked)

The Creative NZ form asks three questions. Ngāika answered them in
this exact order:

| Q | Heading | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What development activity or activities do you want to do? | ~360 words |
| 2 | What is your plan to deliver the development activity or activities? | ~330 words |
| 3 | What are the benefits to your arts organisation or group or your communities? | ~360 words |

Plus front matter: **Executive Summary** (~150 words) and **Strategic
Context** (~200 words). Plus back matter: **Indicative Budget** table
and **Conclusion** with a whakataukī.

**Phase structure in Q2:** Phase I (months 1–3) Assessment → Phase II
(months 4–9) Upskilling → Phase III (months 10–12) Codification. This
"assess → train → embed" arc is the spine.

## Voice and tone (the style guide for future drafts)

**Register:** Formal-policy-meets-kaupapa. Not academic, not casual.
Sentences are long but disciplined. Reads like a serious funder
submission, not a band bio.

**Vocabulary that worked:**

- Te Tiriti o Waitangi, te ao Māori, tikanga, rangatiratanga,
  kaitiakitanga, mana whenua
- tāngata whaikaha Māori (Māori with disabilities — culturally
  specific term, not just "disabled Māori")
- Tino Rangatiratanga
- Te Kaa, Te Tiriti (programme names from Maurea)
- Vibrotactile, haptic-ready, universal design
- Trilingual (English / te reo Māori / NZSL)
- "Māori futurism movement" — **this is the framing line**. Use it.

**Vocabulary to avoid:** anything that reads like a generic arts grant
boilerplate. "Empowering communities," "celebrating culture," "honouring
tradition" — Ngāika did NOT use these.

**Concrete specificity that scored:**

- Named programmes (NZQA Level 4 Bicultural Competency, "Arts For All",
  Te Kaa, Te Tiriti)
- Named organisations (Arts Access Aotearoa, Maurea, Access Advisors,
  WordsWorth Interpreting, Otago Polytechnic, MMIC, SoundCheck Aotearoa)
- Dollar split across 5 budget categories with line-item reasoning
- Concrete deliverable dates (month ranges for each phase)
- 25% statistic (NZ Deaf/disabled population) used to frame market reach

**Cultural touchstones that landed:**

- Closed with whakataukī: *"He aha te mea nui o te ao? He tāngata,
  he tāngata, he tāngata."* — translated. This was the final line of
  the entire document. Powerful move.
- Referenced by name the Māori Music Industry Collective (MMIC) and
  SoundCheck Aotearoa — showing Ngāika is plugged into the sector.

## Budget structure (what to mirror)

5 categories, all amounts rounded:

| Category | $ | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Professional Fees | $4,000 | Cultural + accessibility consultants, 20 hours each |
| Training and Education | $1,800 | NZQA Level 4 Bicultural Certificate |
| Digital and Tech Development | $2,200 | Website audit + haptic software trials |
| Community Engagement | $1,500 | Focus group koha, catering, NZSL interpreters |
| Materials and Resources | $500 | Bilingual/trilingual training manuals |
| **Total** | **$10,000** | Maximum grant |

**Pattern:** ~40% professional fees, ~18% training, ~22% tech, ~15%
community, ~5% materials. Skews toward paying external expertise
(consultants, training providers) over internal — funders like
seeing money go to capability, not salaries.

> "Two core pillars: Cultural Competency and Accessibility for Deaf

## Strengths to copy in future applications

1. **Two-pillar framework** — clear, parallel, memorable. Funders
   love it. Build your next pitch around two named pillars.
2. **Phase structure** — "assess → train → embed" reads as
   competent and realistic.
3. **Concrete deliverables** — every claim is anchored to a date,
   dollar, or named partner.
4. **Cultural grounding + universal design** — the dual accessibility
   framing (Māori + Deaf/disabled) was the unique angle. Don't lose
   it in future applications.
5. **Sector integration** — naming MMIC, SoundCheck Aotearoa, NZQA
   shows Ngāika isn't operating in isolation.
6. **Whakataukī close** — powerful, traditional, and translated.

## Weaknesses / room for next time

- **No mention of music releases or waiata** — this was a capability
  grant, so that's appropriate. But future production grants should
  foreground the actual music.
- **No evaluation metrics** — how will we know it worked? Funders
  increasingly want measurable outcomes (e.g. "X staff certified by
  month 9").
- **Risk section absent** — what if a consultant pulls out? Future
  applications could add a brief risk register.
