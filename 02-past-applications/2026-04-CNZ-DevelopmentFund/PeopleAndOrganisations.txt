ANAMATA RECORDS — ORGANIZATIONS AND GROUPS DEVELOPMENT FUND APPLICATION 2026
People and Organisations Involved
Key personnel and partners engaged in the delivery of the development programme
The following individuals and organisations have been identified as essential contributors to the 12-month capability-building
programme. Each party brings specialist expertise directly relevant to the two core pillars of this initiative: Cultural Competency and
Accessibility for Deaf and disabled communities. Anamata Records is currently in active contact with all parties listed below.
Stace Robertson
ARTS ACCESS AOTEAROA
Role
Lead Accessibility Advisor
Status
Currently in contact — engaged to facilitate the baseline accessibility audit and "Arts For All" framework
implementation
Stace Robertson serves as Lead Accessibility Advisor (Kaiārahi a Toi) at Arts Access Aotearoa, where he is the national lead for helping arts
organisations become more inclusive for Deaf and disabled communities. His expertise in the "Arts For All" framework makes him the ideal
partner to guide our initial audit and establish a robust accessibility baseline for Anamata Records.
Precious Clark
MAUREA
Role
Māori Cultural Consultant
Status
Currently in contact — engaged to provide Te Tiriti-led governance advisory and bicultural competency training
for staff
Precious Clark leads Maurea, a Māori cultural consultancy specializing in embedding Te Ao Māori into organizational frameworks. Through their
"Te Kaa" and "Te Tiriti" programmes, Maurea works with organizations to ensure that Māori values are not merely acknowledged but genuinely
integrated into governance and everyday practice. Precious will guide Anamata Records through the process of reviewing and rewriting our
contracts, policies, and operational language to reflect our Te Tiriti obligations.
Dr. Chandra Harrison
ACCESS ADVISORS
Role
Digital Accessibility Consultant
Status
Currently in contact — engaged to conduct the technical website audit and haptic software accessibility trials
Dr. Chandra Harrison is the Managing Director of Access Advisors, a New Zealand digital consultancy specializing in universal design and web
accessibility standards. Her technical expertise will be critical in assessing and improving the accessibility of our digital platforms, and in
guiding the technical trials of vibrotactile and haptic software as part of our Haptic-Ready mix protocol development.

WordsWorth Interpreting
NZSL INTERPRETING AGENCY
Role
NZSL Agency
Status
Currently in contact — engaged to provide qualified NZSL interpreters for wānanga and trilingual content
production
WordsWorth Interpreting is a leading New Zealand Sign Language interpreting agency that connects organisations with qualified interpreters
for arts and community engagement contexts. Their involvement will ensure that all wānanga, focus groups, and community consultation
sessions are fully accessible to Deaf participants, and that our trilingual resources in English, Te Reo Māori, and NZSL are produced to a
professional standard.
Wairere Iti
SOUNDCHECK AOTEAROA / MĀORI MUSIC INDUSTRY COLLECTIVE (MMIC)
Role
Industry Advisor
Status
Currently in contact — engaged for mentorship regarding Māori music industry standards and "Safer Spaces"
protocols
Wairere Iti is a central figure in the Māori Music Industry Collective (MMIC) and a Board Member for SoundCheck Aotearoa, where he focuses on
equitable representation and Māori leadership in music. His mentorship will provide Anamata Records with invaluable industry-specific
guidance, ensuring our capability-building work is grounded in the realities of the Māori music sector and aligned with established "Safer
Spaces" frameworks.
Otago Polytechnic
TRAINING PROVIDER
Role
Training Provider
Status
Currently in contact — regarding staff enrollment in the NZQA Level 4 Certificate in Bicultural Competency
Otago Polytechnic is an accredited provider of the NZQA Level 4 Certificate in Bicultural Competency, a programme designed to help
professionals embed Māori values and tikanga into their workplace practice. Staff enrollment in this qualification forms a cornerstone of Phase
II of our development programme, ensuring that the bicultural competency we build is formally recognized and nationally credentialed.
Ngāika Smith
ANAMATA RECORDS
Role
Project Manager and Lead Researcher
Responsibilities
Overseeing delivery of all three programme phases, managing the $10,000 budget, and leading the technical
development of the Haptic-Ready protocol
Ngāika Smith is a researcher and music producer with a focus on Ngāti Kahungunu and Ngāti Awa history, and a dedicated commitment to the
Māori futurism movement in Aotearoa's music sector. As Project Manager and Lead Researcher, Ngāika will coordinate all consultants and
training providers, ensure accountability across each phase of the programme, and lead the internal development of the Haptic-Ready mix
protocol.
