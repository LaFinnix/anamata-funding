Executive Summary
Anamata Records is a Māori-led music label operating at the intersection of contemporary sound and traditional
indigenous knowledge. Our name, meaning "the future" or "the time to come," demands proactive engagement with the
systemic barriers that have historically marginalized Māori creatives and Deaf and disabled communities.
This proposal outlines a 12-month organizational capability-building initiative focused on two core pillars: Cultural
Competency and Accessibility. By embedding Te Tiriti o Waitangi principles and universal design into our governance, we
aim to establish a resilient, inclusive organization that leads the Māori futurism movement in Aotearoa's music industry. We
are requesting the maximum grant of NZD $10,000 to fund professional consulting, staff training, digital development, and
community engagement.
Strategic Context
The evolution of the music industry in Aotearoa New Zealand has reached a critical juncture where artistic excellence is
inseparable from ethical stewardship and social equity. Anamata Records bridges the gap between contemporary sounds
and traditional Māori influences. However, the history of Māori music labels reveals that creative success is often
hampered by a lack of robust organizational infrastructure and safeguards against external commercial pressures.
The Creative New Zealand Development Fund provides a targeted mechanism to address these disparities by prioritizing
capability building over direct artistic production. For Anamata Records, this means transitioning from reactive, project-to-
project operations to a sustainable strategy that codifies our values into long-term governance.

Grant Application Responses
The following responses directly address the three required questions for the Organizations and Groups Development
Fund application.
QUESTION 1
What development activity or activities do you want to do?
Anamata Records seeks funding to undertake a comprehensive, 12-month capability-building initiative focused on two
core pillars: Cultural Competency and Accessibility for Deaf and disabled communities. Our primary development activity
is to embed bicultural practices and universal accessibility into our organizational framework, transitioning from reactive
projects to sustainable strategies.
This initiative involves a systematic process of assessment, upskilling, and policy codification. We will engage external
experts, including Māori cultural facilitators and accessibility consultants from Arts Access Aotearoa, to conduct a baseline
gap analysis of our current contracts, marketing, and digital platforms. Following this, we will implement targeted staff
training, enrolling our team in the NZQA Certificate in Bicultural Competency (Level 4) and specialized New Zealand Sign
Language (NZSL) workshops for music. This training will equip our staff with essential skills in te reo Māori, tikanga, and
the cultural nuances of working with Deaf performers.
Furthermore, we will explore the integration of vibrotactile technology to create haptic-supported musical experiences.
Ultimately, these activities will culminate in the development of a formal 5-year Accessibility Plan and an Accessibility
Charter, ensuring that our commitment to inclusivity and Te Tiriti o Waitangi principles is permanently codified into our
governance and operational workflows.
QUESTION 2
What is your plan to deliver the development activity or activities?
Our plan to deliver these development activities is structured across three distinct phases over a 12-month period.
Phase I — Assessment and Auditing (Months 1 to 3): We will contract external cultural and accessibility consultants
to conduct a comprehensive gap analysis. This involves reviewing our current operations, testing digital platforms for
accessibility compliance, and hosting focus groups with tāngata whaikaha Māori to identify specific barriers to their
participation in our music.
Phase II — Staff Upskilling and Training (Months 4 to 9): Our personnel will complete the NZQA Level 4 Bicultural
Competency programme and participate in NZSL workshops tailored for the music industry. Concurrently, we will
conduct technical trials of vibrotactile production methods to develop a prototype Haptic-Ready mix protocol.
Phase III — Policy Codification and Future Strategy (Months 10 to 12): We will synthesize our learnings into
permanent organizational assets. We will establish a Māori and Disabled Advisory Board, finalize and publish our 5-
year Accessibility Plan, and launch our public Accessibility Charter.
The total project budget of $10,000 will be allocated efficiently across professional consulting fees, staff training and
education, digital and technological development, community engagement initiatives, and the creation of bilingual and
trilingual training resources.

QUESTION 3
What are the benefits to your arts organisation or group or your communities?
The proposed capability-building activities will yield profound benefits for Anamata Records, the artists we represent, and
the broader communities we serve.
For our organization, this initiative establishes robust internal infrastructure and long-term financial resilience. By codifying
bicultural competency and universal design into our operations, we mitigate the risk of cultural missteps and ensure
sustainable governance grounded in te ao Māori principles such as rangatiratanga and kaitiakitanga. Furthermore, by
addressing the needs of the Deaf and disabled communities—a demographic encompassing 25% of New Zealanders—we
significantly expand our audience reach and market potential, creating new revenue streams through accessible content.
For our communities, particularly tāngata whaikaha Māori, the benefits are transformative. We are moving beyond
compliance to actively champion human rights, ensuring equitable access to cultural life. By producing trilingual resources
in English, Te Reo Māori, and NZSL, and pioneering vibrotactile musical experiences, we dismantle sensory barriers. This
allows Deaf individuals to fully engage with the rhythm, emotion, and cultural narratives of waiata Māori. This inclusive
approach fosters a profound sense of belonging, reduces social isolation, and actively contributes to community wellbeing,
fulfilling our mission to lead a Māori futurist movement where indigenous art forms thrive in accessible, contemporary
spaces.

Conclusion
The strategic development of Anamata Records through the Creative New Zealand fund represents a commitment to a
future where indigenous knowledge and modern accessibility coexist seamlessly. By building organizational capability in
bicultural competency and Deaf accessibility, we honor the legacies of Māori music labels while charting a new, more
inclusive course.
The move toward trilingual expression, vibrotactile engagement, and Te Tiriti-based governance ensures that Anamata
Records will not only survive rapidly changing times but will thrive as a central pillar of community wellbeing. The future is
not something that happens to us; it is something we create through intentional, culturally grounded, and universally
accessible actions today.
